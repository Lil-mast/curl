# Product

## Problem

People in refugee and underserved communities need education, services, work, and official information. The systems that hold that information are usually websites, English forms, and phone trees. Many people would rather **speak**, often in Somali, and get a **next step**, not a PDF.

## Users

Fill in during **T0.1**. Suggested starting point:

| Role | Need | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary: newly arrived adult | School, work, where to get help | Limited English, phone-only, little time |
| Secondary: young job / scholarship seeker | CV, openings, deadlines | May mix Somali and English |
| Secondary: helper (family, caseworker, teacher) | Use the assistant *with* someone | Needs shareable, accurate answers |

## Launch slice

Fill in during **T0.2**:

- **Place:** _one city / region / camp_
- **Languages:** Somali, English
- **Domains in order:** services → education → jobs → scholarships → CV

## Core flows

1. **Ask for a service** — “Where can I get help with…?” → 1–3 options + who to call + what to bring.
2. **Learn** — explain a topic simply; optional short practice.
3. **Work** — what kinds of jobs, how to look, what employers often ask.
4. **Scholarship** — what exists, stated eligibility, deadline, link/org — never “you are eligible.”
5. **CV by voice** — questions → simple CV text the user can hear and copy. Not stored by default (**T4.11**).

## Voice UX rules

- One primary action: **Talk**.
- Show captions for trust and noisy rooms (**T5.6**).
- Stop / mute is always visible.
- After each answer: one question — “Want me to explain that again?” or “Should I help with the next step?”

## Success metrics (pilot)

Propose in **T0.6**, cap at three. Examples:

- % of sessions with ≥3 voice turns and no typing
- % of replies that stay in the user’s language
- Partner rating: “I would let a family use this” (yes/no)
